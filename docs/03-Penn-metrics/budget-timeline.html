Budget Reduction Timeline

The University of Nebraska – Lincoln’s ‘Metrics’

Published

April 2, 2026

Abstract

In Fall 2025, the University of Nebraska Lincoln announced that there would be 27.5 million dollars of budget cuts, including the closure of several departments and academic programs. The department cuts were (theoretically) motivated by a set of quantitative metrics as well as unspecified qualitative factors. Six departments were proposed for elimination: Educational Administration, Textiles, Merchandising, and Fashion Design, Earth & Atmospheric Science, Statistics, Landscape Architecture, and Community and Regional Planning. Of these, ultimately, the four largest departments were cut, while Landscape Architecture and Community and Regional Planning were merged. Using the data provided to departments and programs, the Statistics department at UNL examined the quantitative metrics, finding several severe errors which undermined the reliability of the decisions made by administration.

During the Academic Planning Committee hearings, Josh Davis (at the time, UNL’s Vice Chancellor for Institutional Strategy and External Relations) read the following into the public record stating the approach as being

transparent and aligned with institutional priorities. The metrics selected for instructional and research performance are grounded in national best practices … These metrics serve as quantitative anchors in a broader process that includes qualitative input from academic leadership.

In this seminar, we will discuss the process, the lack of shared governance, and the problems with administrative metrics used to compare departments within a university system. While we will be discussing statistics, no particular quantitative knowledge is assumed.
